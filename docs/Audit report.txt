#CertiBuy — Implementation Audit

**Audited:** 13 November 2026 · **Build:** the shipped application and its seeded demo dataset.
**Scope:** every route, server module, backend function, entity, shared domain module, integration call and Markdown file in the repository.
**Purpose:** an accurate, evidence-based record of what exists **today**, as the baseline for a rebuild on another stack.

Status vocabulary used throughout: **Implemented** (a real user can complete it end to end, server-authoritative) · **Partial** (present but with a stated gap) · **Simulated** (the flow exists, the external effect is faked) · **Not implemented**.

## 2. Executive summary

- **The custody-first marketplace is real.** Selling, grading, counter inspection, handover, listing, browsing, checkout, escrow bookkeeping, returns, payouts-ledger, liquidity, ladder ageing, exceptions and the ops console are all implemented with server-side rules.
- **Every money *movement* is simulated.** No payment gateway, no bank-transfer rail and no courier API are connected. The ledger, prices, fees and state machines are genuine; the rupees do not move.
- **The "AI" grade is a deterministic rule engine**, not a vision model. It is reproducible and explainable, and it never prices anything.
- **Roles are three, not four.** Seller and buyer are one `customer` account with `active_mode`; technician and ops are staff; platform `admin` is a superset.
- **19 divergences from the proposal** are itemised in §9 — the material ones are: no buyer order cancellation, no seller-cancellation fee, grade D never listed, support requests with no ops inbox, metric set different from the documented one, and two inconsistent `floor_price` definitions.

## 3. Roles as they actually exist

| Persona (proposal) | Account reality | Permission key |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller | `customer` with `active_mode = "seller"` | `app_role = customer` |
| Buyer | `customer` with `active_mode = "buyer"` | `app_role = customer` |
| Inspection technician | separate staff account, `hub_id` required | `app_role = technician` |
| Operations admin | staff account | `app_role = ops`, or platform `role = admin` |

- A customer's two sides are a **view preference**, never an access boundary; guards check `app_role` only.
- First sign-in with an empty `app_role` is bootstrapped to `customer` **server-side**, once.
- Platform `admin` sees everything and is the only role that may write `app_role`, `hub_id`, `trust_score`, `return_count`, `account_flagged`.
- There is **no in-app invitation**: accounts are created through the platform's user management, then assigned a role in Ops → Users.

## 6. Entities (21)

`Settings` (single record, 46 numeric/flag fields — every fee, day count, percentage, cap, weight, threshold) · `DeviceModel` (brand, model, storage, category, colours, image, launch & successor dates, original price, grade A–D market prices, B2B floor, `price_is_placeholder`, liquidity tier, `ic_paused`, `active`) · `LiquidityScore` (score, days to sell, volatility, launch window, source, `is_current`) · `Hub` (type, address, slots, capacity, distance, active) · `Submission` (declared condition, repairs, water, accessories, warranty, photos, retakes, diagnostics, status, chosen option, consent, final grade, mismatch fee) · `Offer` (version, priced band, IC amount, guaranteed price, eligibility + reason, risk %, breakdown, expiry, status) · `GradeAssessment` (estimate, band, confidence, factors, source) · `InspectionReport` (intake/return, checklist, battery, photos, component grades, verdict, sealed/tag/signature, identity checks, quoted/revised, seller decision) · `Booking` (hub, date, slot, status) · `Device` (grade, payout option, acquisition cost, custody, hub, balance paid, parts, return count, status) · `Listing` (base/current/floor price, ladder step, reserved days, override, warranty, status) · `Order` (amounts, delivery, method, status, escrow status, timestamps, refund, replacement) · `EscrowTransaction` · `Payout` (type, amount, trigger, status incl. `due`, attempts, failure reason) · `ReturnRequest` (reason, DOA preference, photos, status, recommendation, outcome, deduction, refund, replacement) · `ExceptionCase` (four types, resolution) · `Notification` (event, title, body, link, read) · `ActivityLog` · `SupportRequest` · `BlacklistedImei` · `User` (app_role, active_mode, phone, city, avatar, hub_id, payout account, delivery address, trust score, return count, flagged).

Access rules exist on every entity: customers see only their own submissions/offers/orders/returns/notifications; technicians are scoped to their `hub_id`; catalogue/liquidity/hubs/settings are publicly readable but admin-writable; exception and activity tables are staff-only.

## 9. Divergences, gaps and inconsistencies

**F1 — Payment capture is simulated (high, expected).** Checkout offers UPI/card/net-banking and a "simulate a declined payment" switch; nothing is charged, no gateway, no webhook, no reconciliation. Escrow is a ledger table, not a provider hold.

**F2 — Payout rail is simulated (high, expected).** `attemptPayout` flips `pending → approved → paid` and only fails when `Settings.simulate_payout_failure` is on. No beneficiary verification, no UTR.

**F3 — Courier is not integrated (medium).** Shipment states are advanced by ops (or by the buyer in demo mode via the prototype control). No AWB, no webhook, no delivery proof.

**F4 — "AI grading" is a rule engine (medium, by design).** Estimate = worst of battery, cosmetic, self-declared function checks; band and confidence from repair certainty and photo retakes. No image analysis. The interface is swappable (see `ai-integration.md.txt`).

**F5 — Photo gate is client-only (medium).** The wizard requires every shot before continuing and validates resolution + brightness in the browser, but `gradeSubmission` never checks the photo array. A direct call can grade a submission with no photos.

**F6 — Roles collapsed (informational).** The proposal's four personas are three `app_role` values plus the platform admin; seller and buyer share `customer` + `active_mode`.

**F7 — No buyer order cancellation (high).** `Order.status` includes `cancelled`, but no command or screen writes it. A buyer can only return after delivery. Consequently the "cancellation" half of the change-of-mind rule does not exist; the ₹500 applies to a **change-of-mind return**, not a pre-dispatch cancellation.

**F8 — No seller-side cancellation fee (medium).** After accepting an offer a seller can cancel the hub slot (`manageBooking`) or decline at the counter (`declineRevised`) at no charge. The only seller-side penalty is the ₹100 mismatch fee after a two-grade drop.

**F9 — Grade D is never listed (medium).** Handover sends grade D to refurbishment and creates no listing, yet the buyer grade guide states D ships without a warranty, and a grade-D **return** is relisted with `warranty_months: 0` (`relistDevice`). Either the guide copy or the relist path should change.

**F10 — Two `floor_price` definitions (medium).** A first listing sets `floor_price = acquisition_cost` (what CertiBuy paid); a relisted return sets `floor_price = model.b2b_floor_price`. The ladder stops at different levels for the same device depending on its history.

**F11 — Customer care has no ops inbox (medium).** The form saves a `SupportRequest` and emails the care address; there is no screen where ops can read, reply to or resolve it (`SupportRequest` count is 0 because nothing consumes it). Ops → Exceptions covers returns/payments, not messages.

**F12 — No in-app invitations (medium).** Ops → Users lists accounts and edits role/hub; accounts must be created through the platform's user management first. `inviteUser` is not called anywhere in the app.

**F13 — Documented metric set was wrong (low).** The implemented nine are: grade-mismatch rate, offer-acceptance rate, Instant Cash share, median days to sell, 30-day sell-through, devices reaching day 21 unsold, inventory ageing beyond the review day, not-as-graded return rate, cost per successful acquisition — each with 8 weekly periods. (Earlier docs listed gross margin, escrow age, payout straight-through and hub throughput; those are **not** implemented. Corrected in `automations.md.txt` and `overview.md.txt`.)

**F14 — Notification email is limited (low).** Uses the platform's transactional mail to registered addresses only; the base URL is hardcoded to the published host; no templates, no digest, failures swallowed silently (by design) but not surfaced anywhere.

**F15 — Activity log has no viewer (low).** Records are written by every job/command and shown only as a recent-activity feed on the ops overview; there is no filtered audit screen.

**F16 — User `return_count` is never incremented (low).** Ops shows and edits it, but only `Device.return_count` is written when a return is raised. The user-level lifetime counter is decorative.

**F17 — Liquidity "demand" is a proxy (low).** There are no views or enquiries, so demand = recent sales ÷ (recent sales + active listings). Documented as if it were a demand signal.

**F18 — No rate limiting or abuse control (low at this scale).** The help form, commands and auth rely on platform defaults; no CAPTCHA, no per-user quotas, no duplicate-submission guard beyond "one open return".

**F19 — Support content is private but unbounded by policy (low).** Message length is validated (10–1000); there is no retention or purge path for photos, signatures or supporters' details.

## 11. Validation and security coverage

Enforced server-side: caller identity and role on every staff endpoint; ownership on every seller/buyer action; hub scoping for technicians; money and status recomputed from records, never accepted from the body; enums and lengths validated; IMEI re-validated (15 digits + Luhn); booking capacity/conflict; return window and single-open-return; offer expiry at accept; handover preconditions (sealed, tag format, signature, no existing device).

Gaps: photo presence (F5), no rate limits (F18), no webhook signature surface yet (nothing to sign), audit viewer (F15), support inbox (F11), buyer cancellation (F7).

## 12. Migration risk register

1. **Money arithmetic is the crown jewel** — port `pricing`, `offer`, `inspection`, `custody`, `returns`, `lifecycle` unchanged and keep `Settings` as the only source of numbers.
2. **Settings must be seeded before any flow** — the engine refuses to price without it (400), it never invents defaults.
3. **Access rules are part of the schema** — recreate them as database policies, especially technician hub scoping and "money tables are system-write-only".
4. **The business date is data** — keep `sim_day_offset` (or an explicit `businessDate` argument) or the demo and the jobs diverge.
5. **Two ledger tables carry the truth** (`Payout`, `EscrowTransaction`) — never delete rows; correct with compensating entries.
6. **Simulated boundaries must be labelled** — payment, payout, courier and AI grading need real providers before production; the interfaces are already isolated.
7. **Decide F7, F9, F10, F11 before rebuilding** — they are product decisions, not implementation details.
